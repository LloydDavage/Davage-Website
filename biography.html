The Life &
Times of Lloyd Davage

Southampton · Architect · Archer · Maker

Nineteen years into a career built on solving hard technical problems, Lloyd Davage has spent equal energy building things outside the office — clubs, community, and the odd 3D-printed jig to hold it all together.

A Southampton Foundation

Lloyd Davage was born and raised with Southampton and Hampshire as home ground, and it remains where he lives and works today. A BSc in Computer Science from the University of Portsmouth gave him the technical grounding for a career that would take him from front-line support desks to senior architecture roles — and along the way he became MBCS, an ITIL Foundation holder, and a graduate of 42 Pluralsight courses and 64 LinkedIn Learning certificates, a habit of continuous learning that runs through everything else he does.

TWO DECADES IN TECHNOLOGY

Building a Career in Technology

Lloyd's professional life began in August 2006 at Scottish & Southern Energy as a Support Analyst Programmer, standing 24-hour watch over Super Prime applications spanning Generation, Trading, Forecasting, and Settlements. It was demanding, unglamorous work — the kind that teaches you how systems really fail — and he used it to lead the Zema Migration Project, integrating a risk management suite into the company's ETRM landscape.

In 2012 he moved into Shell International Trading & Shipping as an Application Developer, building C# applications on MVC, Telerik, and Razor to replace ageing VB6 systems supporting trading, gas, and power operations — work recognised with a Best IT Project award in 2014 for a Capacity Management Tool built for Gas Operations. Three years later he stepped into a Business Analyst role as Operational Landscape Manager, becoming the subject matter expert bridging Trayport, ICE, and N2EX platforms with Shell's Information Management function.

From 2018, Lloyd moved into Shell Treasury as a Senior IT Operations Engineer, delivering secure, reliable services and authoring the complex SQL that underpinned risk and audit investigations across multiple integrated systems. A Treasury Innovation Award followed in 2019 for a Splunk implementation he helped roll out across the department. By 2022 he had been promoted to Senior Solutions Architect within Shell Finance, advising product owners and engineers across Pensions, Insurance, Banking, and Treasury, using threat modelling and enterprise architecture tooling to catch risk before it became incident — work that earned him a VP award in 2023 for the high-profile Quantum Upgrade project.

Since April 2025, Lloyd has been Senior Solutions Architect at SSE Energy Markets, leading DevOps strategy and cloud-native architecture across the organisation's value streams — platform engineering, API integration, and secure-by-design practice, applied at scale.

BEYOND THE OFFICE

Archery, Shooting & Community

In 2019, Lloyd co-founded Ordnance Survey Archers, and has served ever since as Secretary and a qualified Archery GB Level 2 Coach. What started as a small club has grown to over 50 members under his stewardship — he handles the governance and safeguarding as readily as he runs a beginners' course, and competes himself at local, regional, and national level, where the same discipline and composure he brings to enterprise architecture serves him just as well on the shooting line.

He holds the same dual role — Secretary and qualified NSRA Range Officer — at Southampton Small-bore Rifle & Pistol Club, where he also built the club's online membership, booking, and competition management system. His own shooting takes him to the historic ranges at Bisley for .308 fullbore competition, a discipline he approaches with the same curiosity about ballistics, equipment, and technique that defines his engineering work.

MAKING & DIGITAL LIFE

A Maker at Heart

Outside the day job and the club committees, Lloyd's making instinct shows up everywhere: CNC routing on a Genmitsu 4040, 3D design and printing on a Creality Ender S1 with SolidWorks, and a Raspberry Pi-based target camera system he designed so shooters at SSBRC could check their scores without walking the length of the range. He carries the same instinct into his community web development — four production sites, for OS Archers, SBFTC, SSBRC, and SDRPA, each with real members depending on it working properly.
